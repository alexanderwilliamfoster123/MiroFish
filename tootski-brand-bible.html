Prologue — a note to the designer

Everything between the references and the fence is yours to design.

No typefaces named. No hexes given. No layout drawn. What you'll find is the soul, the audience, the world, the references, the voice, the things we never do, and the feeling every piece should leave behind. If unsure, the references win over the brief.

One

“Tootski is the ritual of the sharp, the fast, and the slightly dangerous — a small piece of theatre for people who treat their attention like money.”

What we actually sell

We don't sell nicotine. We sell a ritual.

Marlboro never sold tar; it sold freedom. Playboy never sold paper; it sold taste and permission. Red Bull never sold a drink; it sold a worldview. Tootski sells the most stylish moment of your day — and the quiet signal that you're the kind of person who has them. The strip is the artifact. The feeling is the product.

Stylish. Dangerous‑adjacent.

The most important phrase in this document

The brand lives one step from the edge. Flirting with it, never standing on it.

The edge is what makes us magnetic. Standing on it is what kills us.

Three

For adults who treat their attention like money.

The core

Understood by attitude, not age or income. Allergic to try‑hard. The brand must look like it doesn't need them — which is exactly why they'll chase it.

The aspirational halo

Everyone who wants to feel like the core. We design for the core and let the halo reach up — never design down, or coveted becomes cheap.

Four · The world — place

Always interior. Almost always after‑dark.

The corner office at 8pm. The back of a town car. The hotel bar at midnight. The trading floor at 9:28am. Always the sense that something is about to happen — or just has.

The world — time

1980s financial‑district excess as a temperature, not a costume.

The 9:28am adrenaline played completely straight. Money as texture; ambition with no apology and a great suit. As much a current Manhattan steakhouse, a Mayfair club, a Tokyo whisky bar, as any actual decade.

The world — objects & people

Things with weight and history.

A vintage Cartier tank. A heavy crystal tumbler. A brass key. A folded broadsheet. Polished shoes. Populated by Slim Aarons subjects in modern dress — attractive, successful, mid‑deal, mid‑night‑out, somewhere better to be in five minutes.

The world — light & temperature

Warm and low. A single source.

The light of the lamp, not the office ceiling. The rope is at the door, but the room behind it is warm, generous, human. This separates us from sterile luxury and cold tech.

Seven · The Slim Aarons doctrine

“Attractive people doing attractive things in attractive places.”

— Slim Aarons
The Aarons test — every image, every post
Are the people attractive, successful, magnetic — who the audience wants to be?
Are they doing something the audience wants to be doing — living, not posing?
Are they somewhere the audience wants to be — real, specific, desirable?
Does it make the viewer want to step into the frame?

Yes to all four — on‑brand. Built from attraction and aspiration; never from rejection.

Nine

Short. Dry. Confident to the edge of arrogance.

Tootski speaks like the most interesting person at the party, who is about to leave. Say less. Never explain. Command, don't request. Flirt with the edge; never name it.

“Take one. Run.” “For people in a hurry to win.” “Not for everyone. That's the point.” “Welcome to the inside.” “Gone. As predicted.” “The quiet advantage.”
The brand is the verb

“Did a Tootski before the meeting.” “Tootski up.”

The brand itself becomes the word for the act — the way Google did. Every line treats “Tootski” as a word the reader already uses.

Ten · The ritual — five beats
The Draw
The case appears. Weight in the hand.
The Click
The magnetic clasp opens. The signature sound.
The Lift
A single strip between thumb and forefinger. Held.
The Place
Strip to tongue. Eyes up. The dissolve.
The Run
The clasp closes. Already moving.
Sonic identity

The magnetic click is the sound of the brand.

Captured, mastered, used as the audio bookend on every moving piece — the way a studio sting closes a film. Over time, the click alone should mean Tootski.

Eleventhe rope.
The rope

Belonging matters because others are outside. The brand carries itself like a door that might not open for you — and that posture creates obsession.

Cast the world; never guard the door

We attract by association, never by rejection. Engineered omnipresence among the right people. The door is always open; the world on the other side does the selecting.

Twelve · The fence at the edge of the cliff

What Tootski never does.

  • No drug imitation. The danger is in the attitude, never literal. A sommelier, never a dealer.
  • Never youth‑coded. For grown‑ups. Nothing candy, cartoon, or school‑coded.
  • No medical or drug‑like claims. It earns its reputation; we never assert it.
  • Never identity‑based exclusion. We exclude on taste, never on body or appearance.
  • Never use others' IP. Borrow the temperature of the film, not its property.
  • Never look like an energy drink. No neon, no clinical white, no category clichés.
Thirteen

A fast‑dissolve strip. Placed on the tongue.

Sublingual, water‑free, discreet, theatrical — the closest the legal world has to a designed, performable gesture. Two lines, one ritual.

The Night Line — nicotine

The brand's heart and lead product. UK first. The members‑only top of the building.

The Day Line — caffeine

The reach line, freely marketable. The lobby of the building whose top floors are the Night Line.

The object — the case is half the brand

Designed to be set down before the photo is taken.

A small, weighted, refillable case with a magnetic clasp — a luxury lighter, a heavy cardholder, a piece of jewelry. Camera bait like Rimowa. Make the case worth owning before anyone tastes the product.

One case, day to night

The durable status anchor.

The same case takes either line's refills, carrying a customer from day to night seamlessly. The case is the anchor; the refills are the consumable inside.

Variant coding — each flavour a coded specimen

The trading‑floor route.

Each variant as a ticker — TTSK.A, a stock‑chart line, an ISIN‑style serial, edition numbers, percentages, dates in monospace. The visual language of money applied to a consumable. Collecting them feels like collecting something.

Fourteen · The test — five yeses, then it ships
Does it pass the Aarons test?
Would Marlboro have made it with one mark and no clutter?
Would Red Bull's culture team have made it instead of an ad team?
Does it sound like the most interesting person at the party — about to leave?
Does it stay inside the fence?
